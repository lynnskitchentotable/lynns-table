Sweet & Savoury Breakfast Muffins

Sweet or savoury? Why choose? One colourful tray gives you banana-oat fruit muffins and veggie egg muffins — easy to prep ahead, fun to mix up, and made for busy mornings.

Prep 15–20 minRest 30 min or overnightBake 30–40 min
From Lynn's kitchen

Why I make breakfast this way

Some mornings I want breakfast to be quick and convenient, but I still want to sneak more fruit and vegetables into my day. And if I can make it feel a little playful instead of eating the exact same thing every morning? Even better.

That’s how this two-in-one muffin tray happened: half sweet banana-oat breakfast muffins, half savoury egg muffins.

For the sweet side, I skip flour and use oats instead. I keep the added sweetness light with just a little brown sugar and honey, and I love making every muffin slightly different with fruit and sliced almonds. Breakfast becomes a tiny taste test instead of six identical muffins.

For the savoury side, vegetables are the base. Ham and cheese are optional, but I’ll admit: that’s my favourite version. 😂 I also experimented with hot salami because… it’s one of my favourite things, so naturally I had to see what would happen.

Healthy-ish, colourful, practical, and still fun. That’s much more my style of meal prep.

Cook and eat healthy with joy. ♡

Sweet banana-oat muffins

Flour-free base

  • Rolled oats
  • Milk
  • Egg
  • Plain yogurt
  • Ripe banana, mashed
  • Honey
  • A small amount of brown sugar
  • ½ coffee spoon baking powder (optional)
  • Mixed fruit or berries — try a different fruit in each muffin
  • Sliced almonds
Lynn's note: I keep the added sweetness light and let ripe banana and fruit do most of the work.

Savoury veggie egg muffins

  • Eggs
  • Spinach
  • Corn
  • Red bell pepper
  • Yellow onion
  • Cheese
  • Ham, optional
  • Jalapeño, optional
  • Hot salami, optional spicy variation
Flavor tip: Stir-fry the yellow onion until fragrant, then add the spinach for 1–2 minutes before filling the muffin cups. It’s optional, but worth it.
My favourite: ham + cheese. Skip the meat and keep the cheese modest when you want a lighter version.
Prep-ahead win: Let the oat base and banana mixture rest for 30 minutes, or refrigerate them overnight. Morning-you will be very happy.

Method

1

Start the sweet base

Mix the rolled oats with milk, egg and plain yogurt. In a separate bowl, mash the banana with a little honey and brown sugar.

2

Let it rest

Let both mixtures rest for about 30 minutes so the oats can soften, or cover and refrigerate overnight for an easy morning prep.

3

Make every sweet muffin different

Spoon the oat mixture into greased muffin cups. Add the banana mixture, then top individual muffins with different fruits or berries and sliced almonds.

4

Optional — make the spinach & onion more flavorful

For extra flavor, stir-fry the yellow onion in a pan first until it softens and becomes fragrant. Add the spinach and stir-fry for another 1–2 minutes, just until wilted. You can absolutely skip this step when you’re short on time, but it makes the savoury muffins much more flavorful. Trust me. ♡

5

Build the savoury cups

Divide the spinach and yellow onion mixture, corn and red bell pepper among the remaining greased muffin cups. If you skipped the stir-fry step, simply add the spinach and yellow onion directly. Add ham or hot salami if using.

6

Pour + finish

Beat the eggs and pour them over the vegetables. Finish with cheese and jalapeño if you like a little heat.

7

Preheat + bake together

Preheat the oven to 350–375°F (175–190°C) before the muffin tray goes in. Once the oven is fully preheated, bake for about 30–40 minutes. Every oven runs a little differently, so adjust the baking time as needed and check toward the earlier end of the range. The sweet muffins should be set and the egg muffins cooked through.

One tray, two moods. Make the sweet side fruity and playful; make the savoury side veggie-packed, cheesy, spicy — or all three.
A quick nutrition note: These aren't carb-free. Oats, banana, fruit, honey, brown sugar and corn all contain carbohydrates. I think of the sweet muffins as flour-free, oat-based and lightly sweetened.
